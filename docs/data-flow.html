How messy OSHA records become a sub’s verdict

OSHA’s enforcement files have no company ID, so every fix happens in a named step: the build cleans, scopes and groups the records into a read-only warehouse, and matching later ties those records to each subcontractor a GC enters. Nothing is deleted along the way. Bad values are flagged and broken rows are set aside with a reason.

One name through the ten cleaning rules

The rules remove only noise that never tells two companies apart: case, punctuation, case-number prefixes and legal forms. Trade words, initials, numbers and place words stay, so 84 LUMBER is untouched and BRASFIELD CONSTRUCTION never becomes BRASFIELD & GORRIE.

Three rules the flow never breaks

Flag, don’t delete

Blank penalties stay blank instead of becoming $0, deleted citations are marked, and unmapped hazards go to “other”. Totals always reconcile, and the gate checks that they do.

Split rather than merge

A wrong split costs the GC a little review. A wrong merge can pin someone else’s fatality on a sub. So establishments merge only on identical keys, and companies are assembled per sub, where the GC’s evidence is.

Facts and decisions live apart

The warehouse is rebuilt from source every run. Match decisions sit in Postgres and point at deterministic establishment keys, so a rebuild never erases what a GC confirmed.
